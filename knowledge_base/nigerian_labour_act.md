# Nigerian Labour Act — Key HR Provisions

> Source: Labour Act, Cap L1, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria (LFN) 2004.  
> Last updated to reflect National Minimum Wage (Amendment) Act 2024.

---

## 1. Employment Contract

- Every employer must provide a written statement of employment terms to a worker **within 3 months** of the worker starting.
- The statement must include: job title, nature of work, rate of wages, method of payment, hours of work, leave entitlement, and termination notice periods.
- Workers must sign the contract to indicate acceptance.

---

## 2. Working Hours

- Standard working hours: **8 hours per day**, **40 hours per week** for most categories.
- Overtime work must be compensated at a rate not less than **1.25× the normal hourly rate**.
- Workers are entitled to at least **24 consecutive hours of rest** each week (typically Sunday).

---

## 3. Leave Entitlements

### Annual Leave
- Workers are entitled to a minimum of **6 working days** of annual leave after 12 months of continuous employment.
- Workers under 16 years: minimum **12 working days** per year.
- Leave must be taken; it cannot simply be paid out in lieu unless employment ends.

### Sick Leave
- Workers are entitled to **paid sick leave** upon production of a medical certificate.
- Typical policy: 12 days sick leave per year (the Act does not specify a fixed number; this varies by contract).

### Maternity Leave
- Female workers are entitled to a minimum of **12 weeks** (3 months) of maternity leave **with full pay**.
- Maternity leave may begin up to 6 weeks before the expected delivery date.
- No female worker may be dismissed or have notice of dismissal served on her during maternity leave.

### Paternity Leave
- The Labour Act does **not** currently mandate paternity leave. Provision is at employer discretion.
- Some progressive Nigerian employers offer 5–10 working days as company policy.

---

## 4. Wages and Minimum Wage

- **National Minimum Wage (2024): ₦70,000 per month.**
  - This was signed into law in July 2024, increasing from ₦30,000.
- Wages must be paid **at least monthly** and in legal tender (cash or bank transfer).
- Deductions from wages are only permitted for: tax (PAYE), pension contributions, loan repayments (with written consent), and court-ordered deductions.

---

## 5. Termination and Notice Periods

### Notice Requirements
| Length of service | Minimum notice required |
|---|---|
| Less than 3 months | 1 day (or 1 day's pay in lieu) |
| 3 months – 2 years | 1 week |
| 2 years – 5 years | 2 weeks |
| 5 years or more | 1 month |

- Nigeria does **not** recognise at-will employment. Employers must have just cause or provide proper notice/pay in lieu.
- Wrongful termination claims are common in Nigerian courts, which expect procedural fairness.

### Redundancy
- Where employment is terminated due to redundancy, the employer must:
  1. Notify the Ministry of Labour
  2. Apply LIFO (last in, first out) principle where possible
  3. Pay all accrued leave and other entitlements

---

## 6. Discrimination Protections

- The Constitution of Nigeria prohibits discrimination based on: community, ethnic group, place of origin, sex, religion, or political opinion.
- The **HIV and AIDS (Anti-Discrimination) Act 2014** prohibits discrimination based on HIV status.
- The **Discrimination against Persons with Disabilities (Prohibition) Act 2018** prohibits discrimination against persons with disabilities.
- **ESCALATION REQUIRED:** Any employee query involving discrimination must be flagged to a qualified HR officer.

---

## 7. Statutory Deductions

Every Nigerian employer must deduct and remit the following:

| Deduction | Rate | Remit to |
|---|---|---|
| PAYE (Pay As You Earn tax) | Progressive, per FIRS rates | Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) |
| Pension — Employer contribution | 10% of monthly emoluments | Pension Fund Administrator (PENCOM) |
| Pension — Employee contribution | 8% of monthly emoluments | Pension Fund Administrator (PENCOM) |
| NSITF | 1% of total payroll | National Social Insurance Trust Fund |
| ITF | 1% of annual payroll (firms with 5+ staff or ₦50M+ turnover) | Industrial Training Fund |

---

## 8. NYSC (National Youth Service Corps)

- The NYSC Mobilisation Act requires all Nigerian graduates under 30 to complete one year of national service.
- Many Nigerian employers — particularly in the public sector, banks, and large corporates — **require proof of NYSC completion** (discharge or exemption certificate) before confirming employment.
- Failure to comply with NYSC before employment in certain sectors is a statutory offence.
- **Onboarding checklist item:** Request NYSC discharge certificate from graduates under 30.

---

## 9. Data Protection (NDPA 2023)

- The **Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023** governs how employers collect and process employee personal data.
- HR departments must:
  - Collect only data necessary for employment purposes
  - Obtain explicit consent before processing sensitive data (health, biometric)
  - Maintain a data processing register
  - Notify affected employees within 72 hours of a data breach
- **Penalty for non-compliance:** Up to 2% of annual gross revenue or ₦10 million, whichever is higher.

## 10. Common Employee Questions (Pre-answered)

**Q: How many days of annual leave am I entitled to?**  
A: Under the Labour Act, you are entitled to a minimum of 6 working days per year after 12 months of service. Most Nigerian employers offer 15–21 days as company policy.

**Q: Can my employer deduct money from my salary without telling me?**  
A: No. Your employer may only deduct PAYE tax, pension contributions, agreed loan repayments, or court-ordered payments. All deductions must be shown on your payslip.

**Q: What is the minimum wage in Nigeria?**  
A: As of July 2024, the National Minimum Wage is ₦70,000 per month.

**Q: What happens if I am made redundant?**  
A: You are entitled to notice (or pay in lieu), all accrued annual leave, and any other contractual entitlements. Your employer must apply the LIFO principle and notify the Ministry of Labour.

**Q: Am I entitled to maternity leave?**  
A: Yes. Female employees are entitled to 12 weeks of paid maternity leave under the Labour Act. You cannot be dismissed while on maternity leave.
